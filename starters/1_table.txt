1. Hardcoded
Four inputs, four answers, done.

A NAND gate takes two inputs, each either 0 or 1, and gives back one answer. The rule is "not both":

    the output is 1, UNLESS both inputs are 1

NAND is worth knowing. Every chip in every computer you have ever used is built out of these, and nothing else.

There are only four possible inputs, so the laziest thing that could possibly work is to write all four answers down in a table and look them up. Two are filled in. Fill in the other two, and press Run.

When all four pass, the program carries on by itself and puts the gate into a real circuit. Read what happens - that part is the point of the assignment.

Have a think about:

  1. Two inputs needed four rows. How many rows for three inputs? For eight? For thirty-two?
  2. You have known the rule since the top of this page. Is the rule written anywhere in the program?
  3. A real chip has millions of gates in it. Could any of this be done with tables?
